## 1. What Suzuki v2 proves unconditionally

Suzuki constructs the localized self-adjoint Weil operator A_a and proves,
without assuming RH, that it is the Friedrichs extension of the concrete
symmetric operator

B_a = D* G_a D.

The lowest eigenvalue lambda_a is shown to depend continuously on the
localization length a. The paper also proves that lambda_a > 0 for
sufficiently small a.

This validates the repository's abstract spectral-flow split:

small-a positive anchor
+ continuity
+ no positive-scale zero crossing
=> lambda_a > 0 for all a > 0.

The nondegeneracy condition remains RH-equivalent; it is not an independent
proof of it.

## 2. The stronger spectral object

Suzuki's Theorem 1.5 constructs, for every a > 0 and extension parameter
theta, an entire characteristic function W(a,theta;z) whose zeros are exactly
the eigenvalues of a self-adjoint extension of the minimal first-order
differential operator in the Hilbert space generated by the shifted Weil form.

Consequently,

**every zero of W(a,theta;·) is real**

for every finite a, unconditionally.

This is stronger for our purposes than merely knowing that one finite matrix is
positive: it provides a whole family of genuine real-zero entire spectral
functions.

## 3. Suzuki's large-a bridge

Corollary 1.6 isolates the remaining conjectural limit. In the notation of the
paper, if one can choose the extension phase and a non-singular normalization
so that

e^{phi(a,z)} W(a,theta(a);z)
    -> z^2 xi(1/2 - i z) / xi'(1/2 - i z)

uniformly on compact subsets in the required sense as a -> infinity, then the
Riemann Hypothesis follows.

Thus Suzuki's open incoming edge is not finite-a self-adjointness or
real-rootedness. Those are already proved. The hard step is the **large-a
compact-limit identification** with the xi-derived target.

## 4. Exact coordinate match with SOH / PhaseNav

Write

s = 1/2 - i z.

Then

z = i (s - 1/2).

For s = sigma + i t, this gives

z = -t + i (sigma - 1/2).

Therefore

Im z = Re s - 1/2.

The native PhaseNav closure defect is

C(s) = (Re s - 1/2)^2.

Hence, exactly,

C(s) = (Im z)^2.

So Suzuki's statement that the finite-a characteristic functions have only
real zeros is, in the SOH coordinate language, precisely the statement that
their spectral zeros have zero native radial defect.

This coordinate identity is now formalized in Lean as

`halfAxisDefect_eq_suzukiCoord_im_sq`

with

`suzukiCoord(s) = i(s - 1/2)`.

## 5. Crosswalk to reciprocal--conjugation geometry

The projective defect already satisfies

Delta_RC(Omega(s))
    = 4 C(s) / (|s|^2 |1-s|^2).

Combining with the Suzuki coordinate gives

Delta_RC(Omega(s))
    = 4 (Im z)^2 / (|s|^2 |1-s|^2).

Thus three apparently different descriptions are now the same defect:

Suzuki non-real spectral displacement
<-> PhaseNav radial shear
<-> reciprocal--conjugation defect.

All three vanish exactly on the Riemann critical line.

## 6. New proof-search decomposition

## 9. Pole audit update

A separate audit,
\`SOH_SUZUKI_COROLLARY_1_6_POLE_AUDIT_V0_1.md\`,
records an important domain issue with the literal global form of the
Corollary 1.6 target.  The ratio

\[
z^2\xi(1/2-iz)/\xi'(1/2-iz)
\]

is naturally meromorphic because zeros of the denominator that are not xi
zeros create genuine poles.  Consequently the repository does **not** promote
ordinary uniform convergence on every compact subset of all of \(\mathbb C\)
as the working proof target.

The retained Route B target is local: convergence/zero-attraction on
pole-free neighborhoods of xi zeros, with a holomorphic non-vanishing
normalization.  The Lean theorem in \`SuzukiLimit.lean\` already uses only
this pointwise zero-attraction endgame.
