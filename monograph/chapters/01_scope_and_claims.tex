\chapter{Scope, Method, and Claim Discipline}
\label{chap:scope}

\section{The problem being addressed}

The Riemann Hypothesis states that every non-trivial zero $\rho$ of the Riemann zeta function has real part $1/2$ \citep{riemann1859,clay2026}. It is important to distinguish this global assertion from the much simpler observation that the completed zeta function possesses a symmetry centred at $1/2$. A symmetry centre does not generally force every zero to be fixed by the symmetry. It may only force zeros to occur in symmetric families.

The foundational information-spinor layer asks whether the axis $\Re s=1/2$ can be characterized simultaneously as:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the fixed set of the conjugate-affine zeta involution;
  \item the unique point of binary informational balance;
  \item the unique equal-gain two-channel state permitting exact destructive cancellation;
  \item the unique value in $(0,1)$ producing the spinorial sign $-1$ under the phase law $e^{2\pi i\sigma}$.
\end{enumerate}
Each item can be proved exactly once the relevant definitions are fixed. The difficult question is whether the zeros of $\xi(s)$ admit a canonical representation in which these structures are not merely parallel but are linked by a proved analytic mechanism. Later parts of the monograph develop additional quotient, kernel, total-positivity, and no-go routes that do not depend on completion of the initial state-map ansatz.

\section{Variables and domains}

Throughout the monograph,
\[
 s=\sigma+it,\qquad \sigma,t\in\R,
\]
and the open critical strip is
\[
 \Sstrip=\{s\in\C:0<\Re s<1\}.
\]
The critical line is
\[
 \Crit=\{s\in\C:\Re s=1/2\}.
\]
The word ``binary'' refers to a normalized pair of non-negative weights $(\sigma,1-\sigma)$. No claim is made that the zeta function is literally a two-state physical system. The information-spinor ansatz asks whether a two-channel representation can be constructed canonically from the analytic data.

The natural logarithm is denoted by $\ln$. Entropies are therefore measured in nats, so that the balanced binary entropy is $\ln2$. The complex power $2^s$ means $\exp(s\ln2)$, with the real logarithm of $2$.

\section{Autonomy and non-circularity}

A construction intended to illuminate the Riemann Hypothesis must not encode the answer in its definitions. There are several ways circularity can enter:
\begin{itemize}
  \item defining a state only at already known zeros and choosing its weights to be $1/2$;
  \item using a phase function that is set to $\pi$ precisely when $\xi(s)=0$;
  \item dividing by $\xi(s)$ and then interpreting a singular normalization as a zero detector;
  \item fitting parameters to a finite list of tabulated zeros;
  \item invoking self-adjointness without constructing a domain, operator, and spectral equivalence.
\end{itemize}
The canonical bridges demanded later in this text are designed to exclude these manoeuvres.

\begin{definition}[Canonical for the present programme]
A map, operator, or kernel is called \emph{canonical} if it is determined by previously specified zeta, theta, Hilbert-space, coefficient, or symmetry data; is defined on an open domain rather than only on the zero set; is covariant under the relevant involutions when covariance is claimed; contains no parameters fitted to zero locations; and admits an independently checkable rule for its normalization.
\end{definition}

This definition is intentionally demanding. A non-canonical map can always be manufactured to send a prescribed set to any desired geometric locus. Such manufacture has no explanatory force in the proof programme.

\section{Foundational claim classes}

The repository began with five foundational claims. Three are elementary lemmas:
\begin{align*}
\text{SOH-L001:}&\quad \Hbin(\sigma)\text{ is uniquely maximal at }\sigma=1/2,\\
\text{SOH-L002:}&\quad \Acomp(\sigma,\phi)=0\iff \sigma=1/2,\ \phi\equiv\pi\pmod{2\pi},\\
\text{SOH-L003:}&\quad \operatorname{Fix}(\J)=\Crit,
\end{align*}
where $\J(s)=1-\overline{s}$ is the conjugate-affine involution of Chapter~3. One foundational claim is the open bridge:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{SOH-C001.} A canonical zeta-state map converts every non-trivial zero into exact complementary cancellation.
\end{quote}
The corresponding conditional result is:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{SOH-T001.} Under SOH-C001 and the specified normalization, all non-trivial zeros lie on $\Crit$.
\end{quote}

These claims remain part of the monograph, but they are no longer the complete claim ledger. Chapters~16--45 add independent arithmetic, quotient, kernel, no-go, and coefficient results. The current cumulative ledger is Appendix~\ref{app:claims}.

\section{What would count as success?}

There are several levels of success, and they should not be confused.

\paragraph{Level 1: structural explanation.}
One proves that complementarity, entropy, equal-gain cancellation, spinorial sign, and zeta symmetry select the same half-coordinate inside their declared representations. This level is achieved.

\paragraph{Level 2: canonical zero representation.}
For the specific information-spinor route, one constructs a state from classical zeta data such that its detector vanishing is equivalent to $\xi(s)=0$ and its channel norms are linked canonically to $(\sigma,1-\sigma)$. This state-map requirement remains open. Later chapters do construct different canonical objects---notably the quotient entire function $F$, the exact positive Riemann kernel, and coefficient-normalized probability laws---but those constructions do not retroactively prove SOH-C001.

\paragraph{Level 3: zero-forcing analytic mechanism.}
One proves a non-circular property strong enough to force the required zero location. In the quotient branch this may be real-rootedness or a sufficient total-positivity theorem; in the Weil branch it is full admissible positivity. The monograph proves PF$_2$ and several zero-exclusion theorems, but PF$_3$, PF$_\infty$, SOH-G003, and SOH-C005 remain open.

\paragraph{Level 4: accepted proof.}
Every analytic detail, domain issue, convergence statement, and equivalence is verified independently and accepted through ordinary mathematical publication and review. Nothing in this monograph claims that level.

\section{Why retain a conditional theorem?}

A conditional theorem is useful when it compresses an unresolved problem into a precise construction target. The Hilbert--P\'olya strategy is valuable for exactly this reason: if the zero ordinates are the spectrum of a self-adjoint operator, they are real, and the Riemann Hypothesis follows. The hard part is not the final spectral implication but the operator construction \citep{berrykeating1999,connes1999,dlmf2026}.

The information-spinor theorem developed in Chapters~8--9 plays an analogous role for its own branch. It does not replace Hilbert--P\'olya, the later quotient programme, or the Weil criterion. Its value is measured by whether it states the missing assumptions explicitly and rules out vague analogies.
