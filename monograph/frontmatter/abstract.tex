\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This monograph studies why the real value $1/2$ is structurally distinguished in the analytic geometry surrounding the Riemann zeta function while maintaining a strict separation between exact mathematics, finite numerical evidence, model assignments, conditional implications, and open proof obligations.

The elementary layer establishes several exact selections of the half. Binary Shannon entropy is uniquely maximal at $\sigma=1/2$ with value $\ln2$. A normalized equal-gain two-channel amplitude vanishes exactly at equal weights and relative phase $\pi$. The conjugate-affine involution $K(s)=1-\overline{s}$ has fixed set $\Re s=1/2$, and the corresponding declared binary-spinor geometry places balanced states on the Bloch equator. The Dirichlet eta function supplies a classical bridge between alternating sign, $\ln2$, and the non-trivial zeta zeros without determining their horizontal location.

The native PhaseNav construction develops paired theta channels, a Hermite dense core for the Weil quadratic form, reciprocal compactification of prime-power tails, certified finite-section tail bounds, and a basis-adaptive cutoff theorem. The Li/Weil integration then places the projective coordinate $\Omega(s)=s/(1-s)$, Li's coordinate $1-1/s=-1/\Omega(s)$, local reciprocal-quartet growth, and the global Li and Weil criteria in one audited chain. The global Weil positivity claim SOH-C005 remains an RH-equivalent open frontier rather than an assumed lemma.

A second analytic line passes to the even quotient
\[
 \xi\!\left(\frac12+z\right)=F(z^2).
\]
The monograph derives the exact positive Riemann kernel, proves strict log-concavity of its compactified weight, and records Avi Gershon's independent prior 2026 proof of strict log-concavity of the classical Xi kernel. A classical normalized-moment theorem then gives PF$_2$ for the Taylor coefficients of $F$. The PF$_3$ problem is reduced exactly to a ratio-curvature inequality, but PF$_3$, PF$_\infty$, and real-rootedness of $F$ remain open.

The inverse-boundary and spinorial line is also resolved more sharply than in earlier versions. The canonical negative inversion admits an exact Klein-four projective algebra and a quaternion $Q_8$ Pauli/SU(2) lift. However, its proposed spectral action is ruled out: the closed quotient disk $|w|\le1/4$ is zero-free, and the negative inversion maps no zero of $F$ or $\xi$ to another zero. Thus the operator geometry remains exact while global zero-set invariance is excluded.

The coefficient frontier is governed by the unique radius $R_\star>0$ satisfying $F(R_\star)=2F(0)$. On $|w|\le R_\star$ the quotient has strictly positive real part. After this canonical rescaling, its positive coefficients form a probability law with exactly half of the mass at index zero and half on the positive indices; PF$_2$ is preserved, the masses decrease strictly, and a sharpened coefficient envelope follows. SOH-G021 then proves an exact structural no-go: those G020 properties, even taken together, do not imply PF$_3$. An explicit half-mass PF$_2$ probability sequence satisfying the same monotone envelope has G006 ratio-curvature margin $-1271/2500$ and therefore a negative solid order-three Toeplitz minor.

The resulting proof boundary is explicit. The monograph proves the stated geometric, kernel, PF$_2$, zero-exclusion, coefficient-normalization, and G021 insufficiency results, and it closes several invalid shortcuts. It does not prove or disprove PF$_3$ for the actual coefficients of $F$, and it does not prove PF$_\infty$, real-rootedness of $F$, SOH-G003, SOH-C005, or the Riemann Hypothesis.
