The number $1/2$ is the exact centre of the complement $s\mapsto1-s$ and the fixed line of the conjugate-affine involution $s\mapsto1-\bar s$. In the declared normalized binary model, it is also the unique point of maximum Shannon entropy, the unique equal-magnitude condition for two-channel destructive interference, the Bloch equator, and the unique phase fraction producing the sign $-1$ under $e^{2\pi i\sigma}$.

These facts are exact within their stated representations. They can be summarized by the binary-spinor equivalence theorem:
\begin{align*}
 \text{self-complementarity}
 &\iff \text{maximum binary entropy}\\
 &\iff \text{spinorial sign}\\
 &\iff \text{phase-locked cancellation}\\
 &\iff \sigma=\frac12.
\end{align*}
The Dirichlet eta function supplies a separate exact analytic bridge:
\[
 \eta(1)=\ln2,
 \qquad
 \eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s),
\]
and eta and zeta have the same zeros in the open critical strip. Binary alternation is therefore present in the analytic object whose zeros are at issue, but this fact alone does not convert an eta zero into normalized two-channel cancellation.

What Chapters~1--15 do not prove is the implication from an eta or xi zero to the normalized information-spinor cancellation event. The infinite alternating series can cancel without reducing to two equal-norm components. The odd and even analytic channels both vanish at a zeta zero and therefore do not define a normalized state there. Functional symmetry can pair off-axis zeros without fixing them. Entropy selects a line but not the zero ordinates. The phase law supplies the declared sign but not a canonical zeta state map.

Within this initial branch, the unresolved bridge can be stated cleanly: construct an analytic, involution-covariant, non-vanishing Hilbert-space vector whose fixed detector projection equals $\xi(s)$ up to a non-zero factor, and whose complementary component norms are $\Re s$ and $1-\Re s$ in a canonical swap-invariant metric. If all hypotheses of SOH-T001 are independently established, the cancellation lemma gives RH. The theorem is conditional; the state-map construction remains open.
