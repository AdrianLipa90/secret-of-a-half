\chapter{The Universal Relational Zero Law}
\label{chap:relational-zero-triad}

\section{Purpose}

This chapter removes the last unnecessary multiplicity from the zero-axis
construction.  There is one foundational axiom.  Complement normalization,
the Shannon/KL defect, the relational action cost, and the Lagrange-node
conditions are derived structures.

\section{The sole axiom}

\begin{axiom}[A0: universal relational zero]
\label{ax:rz-zero}
Zero has no independent realization.  For every admitted observable, zerohood
is realized as vanishing of its canonical relational defect.
\[
\boxed{
R=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak D_{\rm rel}[R]=0.
}
\]
For an admitted completed-zeta zero \(\rho\), the specialization is
\[
\boxed{
\xi(\rho)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E_{\rm rel}(\rho)=0.
}
\]
\end{axiom}

A0 is the only axiom used in the terminal proof architecture.  It is stated as
a universal law of relation, not as a model-specific energy assumption.

\section{Normalized complement is a definition}

Given a non-degenerate two-sided relation with affine endpoints \(a\ne b\),
define its normalized contrast coordinate
\[
\sigma=\frac{x-a}{b-a}.
\]
The endpoints become \(0\) and \(1\), and exchange of the two sides is
represented by
\[
J(\sigma)=1-\sigma.
\]

No additional axiom is introduced here; this is affine normalization of a
binary contrast.

\begin{theorem}[SOH-RZ001: unique relational fixed point]
\label{thm:rz-fixed-point}
The exchange map has exactly one fixed point:
\[
J(\sigma)=\sigma
\iff
\sigma=\frac12.
\]
With centered coordinate
\[
x_{\rm rel}:=\sigma-\frac12,
\]
this is equivalent to
\[
x_{\rm rel}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Solve \(1-\sigma=\sigma\).  Then \(2\sigma=1\), hence
\(\sigma=1/2\).  Uniqueness follows immediately.
\end{proof}

Thus the normalized relational barycenter and centered zero are the same
point.

\section{Information defect is derived}

Define binary entropy
\[
H_2(\sigma)
=
-\sigma\ln\sigma-(1-\sigma)\ln(1-\sigma)
\]
and the information defect
\[
D_H(\sigma)
=
\ln2-H_2(\sigma).
\]
Direct algebra gives
\[
D_H(\sigma)
=
D_{\rm KL}\!\left(
(\sigma,1-\sigma)
\middle\|
(1/2,1/2)
\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[SOH-RZ002: Shannon/KL zero]
\label{thm:rz-shannon}
For \(0<\sigma<1\),
\[
D_H(\sigma)\ge0,
\]
with
\[
D_H(\sigma)=0
\iff
\sigma=\frac12.
\]
Moreover
\[
D_H'(\sigma)=\ln\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma},
\qquad
D_H''(\sigma)=\frac1{\sigma(1-\sigma)}\ge4.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
D_H(\sigma)\ge2\left(\sigma-\frac12\right)^2.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
KL divergence is non-negative and vanishes exactly when its two arguments
coincide.  The derivative identities follow by differentiation.  Since
\(D_H(1/2)=D_H'(1/2)=0\) and \(D_H''\ge4\), strong convexity yields the stated
quadratic lower bound.
\end{proof}

Therefore Shannon theory does not define the relational zero.  It independently
detects and penalizes displacement from the zero already forced by the
exchange relation.

\section{Relational action defect is derived}

For a finite or countable family of relations let \(F_e\) be each local
dynamical vector in a positive-definite metric \(G_e\), with
\(w_e,\lambda_e>0\).  Define
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak S_{\rm rel}
=
\sum_e
w_e
\left[
\|F_e\|_{G_e}^2
+
\lambda_eD_H(\sigma_e)
\right].
}
\]

This is a derived non-negative cost functional.  It is not the ordinary
Lorentzian action \(S=\int L\,dt\), and \(\mathfrak S_{\rm rel}=0\) must not
be confused with the variational statement \(\delta S=0\).

\begin{theorem}[SOH-RZ003: Relational Lagrange--Zero Theorem]
\label{thm:rz-lagrange}
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak S_{\rm rel}=0
\iff
\forall e:\quad
F_e=0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sigma_e=\frac12.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every summand is non-negative.  A finite or countable sum of non-negative
terms is zero exactly when every term is zero.  Positive definiteness gives
\(\|F_e\|_{G_e}^2=0\iff F_e=0\), and
\Cref{thm:rz-shannon} gives
\(D_H(\sigma_e)=0\iff\sigma_e=1/2\).
\end{proof}

A \emph{Lagrange node} in this algebra means \(F_e=0\): a zero of the declared
relational dynamical vector.  The term does not assert that every
celestial-mechanics Lagrange point is the geometric midpoint of its primaries.

\section{Universality over relation graphs}

For any finite or countable relation graph,
\[
\operatorname{Fix}(J_E)
=
\prod_{e\in E}
\operatorname{Fix}(J_e)
=
\prod_{e\in E}\left\{\frac12\right\}.
\]
Graph topology changes which relations are present, but it does not move the
normalized fixed point of any relation/complement contrast.

Accordingly,
\[
\boxed{
0_{\rm relational}
\equiv
\frac12_{\rm complement}
\equiv
H_2=\ln2
\equiv
D_H=0.
}
\]

\section{Riemann specialization}

For
\[
s=\sigma+it,
\]
the completed-zeta conjugate-affine symmetry acts horizontally as
\[
\sigma\longmapsto1-\sigma.
\]
Its fixed axis is
\[
\Re s=\frac12.
\]

A0 is stronger than symmetry alone: it states that an actual zero of the
admitted observable is realized as zero relational defect, not merely that
zeros occur in symmetric pairs.

Thus for a non-trivial zero \(\rho\),
\[
\xi(\rho)=0
\stackrel{\mathrm{A0}}{\Longrightarrow}
E_{\rm rel}(\rho)=0.
\]
Chapter~\ref{chap:zero-critical-relational-axis} supplies three complementary
derivations showing that \(E_{\rm rel}=0\) forces the Riemann critical axis.
