## 2. Source audit: the odd Fourier channel carries an imaginary unit

Dimitrov and Xu, *Wronskians of Fourier and Laplace Transforms*, arXiv:1606.05011, published in *Transactions of the AMS* 372 (2019), 4107–4125, DOI `10.1090/tran/7809`, define the Fourier transform by

\[
\mathcal F\mu(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ixt}\,d\mu(t).
\]

For a real even kernel \(K\), set

\[
f(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}K(t)e^{-izt}\,dt,
\qquad z=x+iy.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{f(x+iy)-f(x-iy)}2
=\int_{\mathbb R}\sinh(yt)K(t)e^{-ixt}\,dt.
\]

Because \(f\) is real entire,

\[
f(x-iy)=\overline{f(x+iy)},
\]

so the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F[\sinh(y\,\cdot)K](x)
=i\,\Im f(x+iy).
}
\]

Equivalently, the transform of a real odd function under the declared \(e^{-ixt}\) convention is purely imaginary.

In the accessible arXiv text, equation (3.7) uses the same \(e^{-iuz}\) convention, while the displayed formula for the imaginary part and Lemma 3.3 identify the odd `sinh` channel with a real function without the required imaginary factor. This is a convention/sign inconsistency in the checked text. No claim is made here about author intent; the SOH consequence is simply that the historical external transport cannot be used without re-derivation.

The present web/source audit did not locate an erratum. That absence is not evidence that none exists.

## 3. Correct two-body Jensen correlation

Define

\[
Q_y(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}(t-2s)^2
\cosh\!\bigl(y(t-2s)\bigr)
K(t-s)K(s)\,ds.
\]

Set

\[
t=2u,\qquad s=u-r.
\]

Then

\[
t-2s=2r,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_y(2u)=4J_y(u),
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
J_y(u)=\int_{\mathbb R}r^2\cosh(2yr)
K(u+r)K(u-r)\,dr.
}
\]

Direct double-integral expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat Q_y(x)=2H_f(x+iy),
}
\]

and the centered form is

\[
\boxed{
4\widehat J_y(2x)=H_f(x+iy).
}
\]

Thus the kernel previously called the **internal tilt** is not auxiliary: it is the direct kernel representation of the Hermitian Jensen functional.

## 4. External bilinear object remains algebraically distinct

The historical external object is

\[
E_f(z)=\Re\!\left(f'(z)^2-f(z)f''(z)\right).
\]

It is represented by the external tilt

\[
D_y(u)=\cosh(2yu)C(u),
\qquad
C(u)=\int r^2K(u+r)K(u-r)\,dr.
\]

The corrected Jensen object instead uses \(J_y\), with the hyperbolic factor on the **relative coordinate** \(r\):

\[
D_y(u)\ne J_y(u)
\quad(y\ne0\text{ in general}).
\]

For \(f=A+iB\),

\[
H_f-E_f
=2\left[(\Im f')^2-(\Im f)(\Im f'')\right].
\]

Hence no Hermitian-to-bilinear identification is available without an additional theorem.

## 5. N-body bridge: Jensen curvature is a Wick-rotated susceptibility

Let

\[
M(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb R}K(t)e^{\lambda t}\,dt
=f(i\lambda),
\]

and define the two-body partition function

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Z(\alpha,\beta)
=\frac12M(\alpha+\beta)M(\alpha-\beta).
}
\]

Analytically continue the center coordinate by

\[
\alpha=-ix,
\qquad
\beta=y\in\mathbb R.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Z(-ix,y)
=\frac12f(x+iy)f(x-iy)
=\frac12|f(x+iy)|^2.
}
\]

Differentiating twice in the relative coordinate gives Jensen's curvature exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\partial_y^2\mathcal Z(-ix,y)
=|f'(x+iy)|^2
-\Re\!\left(f''(x+iy)\overline{f(x+iy)}\right)
=H_f(x+iy).
}
\]

Therefore the active Jensen quantity is the **Wick-rotated relative susceptibility of the same two-body partition function** already generated by the positive theta kernel.

This removes the former proof obligation "Hermitian \(\to\) bilinear rigidity". The Hermitian quantity is obtained directly from the N-body object.

\[

## 7. Smooth Laguerre–Pólya adversarial control

To ensure that the external/Hermitian separation is not an artifact of the piecewise-linear tent kernel, define

\[
\boxed{
F(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\sin(2^{-n}z)}{2^{-n}z}.
}
\]

Each factor is the Fourier transform of the normalized uniform density on
\([-2^{-n},2^{-n}]\). Their infinite convolution is therefore a nonnegative even probability measure supported in \([-1,1]\).

The product decays on the real axis faster than every inverse power: for the factors with \(2^{-n}|x|\gtrsim1\), the elementary bound \(|\sin w/w|\le1/|w|\) gives a logarithmically increasing number of decay factors. Consequently its inverse Fourier measure has a \(C_c^\infty\) density. In particular the kernel is Schwartz.

Every finite product is in the Laguerre–Pólya class, and compact-uniform convergence follows from

\[
\sum_{n\ge1}4^{-n}<\infty
\]

and the local expansion of `sinc`. Hence \(F\in LP\).

All zeros are real. At

\[
\lambda=4\pi
\]

the factors \(n=1\) and \(n=2\) vanish while the remaining factors do not, so \(F\) has an exact double real zero.

For any real entire

\[
f(z)=(z-\lambda)^m g(z),
\qquad g(\lambda)\ne0,
\]

a direct local expansion at \(z=\lambda+iy\) gives

\[
E_f(\lambda+iy)
=m(-1)^{m-1}g(\lambda)^2y^{2m-2}
+O(y^{2m}),
\]

whereas

\[
H_f(\lambda+iy)
=m(2m-1)g(\lambda)^2y^{2m-2}
+O(y^{2m}).
\]

Thus for the double zero \(m=2\),

\[
\boxed{
E_f(\lambda+iy)<0,
\qquad
H_f(\lambda+iy)>0
}
\]

for all sufficiently small nonzero \(y\).

At \(y=0\), \(E_F(0)=-F(0)F''(0)>0\), and by continuity it remains positive at \(x=0\) for sufficiently small \(|y|\). Therefore for such a fixed nonzero \(y\), the external real transform changes sign between \(x=0\) and a neighborhood of \(4\pi\), and hence has a real zero.

This is a rigorous adversarial obstruction to using the printed external kernel as a general LP/Jensen density criterion, provided the checked theorem is read with the Fourier convention displayed in the same source.
