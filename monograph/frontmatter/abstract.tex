\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This monograph studies why the real value $1/2$ is structurally distinguished in the analytic geometry surrounding the Riemann zeta function while maintaining a strict separation between exact mathematics, finite numerical evidence, model assignments, conditional implications, and open proof obligations.

The elementary layer establishes several exact selections of the half. Binary Shannon entropy is uniquely maximal at $\sigma=1/2$ with value $\ln2$. A normalized equal-gain two-channel amplitude vanishes exactly at equal weights and relative phase $\pi$. The anti-linear involution $K(s)=1-\overline{s}$ has fixed set $\Re s=1/2$, and the corresponding binary spinor geometry places balanced states on the Bloch equator. The Dirichlet eta function supplies a classical bridge between alternating sign, $\ln2$, and the non-trivial zeta zeros without determining their horizontal location.

The native PhaseNav construction develops this structure through paired theta channels, a Hermite dense core for the Weil quadratic form, reciprocal compactification of prime-power tails, certified finite-section tail bounds, and a basis-adaptive cutoff theorem. The identity-axis and holonomy layer then separates transverse displacement from tangent recurrence and records the logical architecture in a typed solver that prevents OPEN or MODEL premises from being silently promoted to proof-level conclusions.

Version 0.9 integrates the projective coordinate
\[
\Omega(s)=\frac{s}{1-s}
\]
with the negative-inverse Li coordinate
\[
z_L(s)=1-\frac1s=-\frac1{\Omega(s)}.
\]
The critical line is mapped exactly to the unit circle in either coordinate. For a reciprocal-conjugate quartet $z=Re^{i\phi}$, the local Li contribution is
\[
L_n=4-2(R^n+R^{-n})\cos(n\phi),
\]
which yields an exact local growth radius $\max(R,R^{-1})$ and an unbounded negative subsequence whenever $R\ne1$. These local facts are then connected to the global Li criterion and to Weil positivity. A separate no-go lemma shows that positive von Mangoldt weights do not make the prime contribution positive semidefinite term by term.

The resulting frontier is explicit: under the standard Weil criterion, the unresolved global task is to prove the arithmetic quadratic form non-negative on the complete admissible test class, equivalently to establish the corresponding global Li positivity, without assuming an RH-equivalent premise. The localized problem may be expressed through the lowest spectral value of a self-adjoint arithmetic Weil operator. The present monograph sharpens and audits that reduction; it does not claim a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.
